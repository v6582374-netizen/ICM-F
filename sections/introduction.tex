\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Background and Motivation}
Generative Artificial Intelligence (Gen-AI) is evolving from a niche auxiliary tool into an indispensable foundational resource across all industries. With its rapidly growing capabilities, Gen-AI is not only optimizing workflows but fundamentally reshaping the task structure of occupations. Correspondingly, higher education must synchronously adjust its training systems to respond to the real-world challenge of ``skill structures being rewritten by technology''~\citep{mughal2025beyondautomationho,wong2025thefutureoflearnin}.

\subsection{Problem Restatement}
The core question posed by the problem is singular: how can post-secondary educational institutions better integrate Gen-AI into their current training programs? This core question decomposes into the following workflow:

First, we need to select one specific occupation from each of the three categories---\textbf{STEM, Trade, and Arts}---and characterize the future changes of these occupations under the influence of Gen-AI. Here, ``future'' can be understood as requiring a defined prediction window, or viewed as a broad time horizon. ``Current trajectory'' can be interpreted as the penetration and adoption rate of Gen-AI in the respective industry; ``expected impact'' refers to the measurable changes this penetration brings to job structures and practitioners' working methods.

Second, after completing the occupation-level analysis, the problem further requires: selecting a ``corresponding specific institution and specific program'' for each occupation, and translating recommendations into concrete improvement measures for these three programs. The requirement is to use mathematical model outputs to provide evidence-based guidance rather than remaining at the level of principled slogans. This aligns with recent higher education research conclusions that institutions need to institutionalize GenAI usage boundaries, learning outcomes, and assessment methods together, rather than merely issuing generalized statements~\citep{hamerman2024aninvestigationofg,jogezai2025generativeartifici}.

Finally, beyond the recommendations given in the problem statement, the team must independently consider additional evaluation dimensions and explain whether the model can be generalized to other institutions or programs, explicitly stating the conditions and boundaries for generalizability.
