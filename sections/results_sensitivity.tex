\section{Simulation Results and Sensitivity Analysis}

\subsection{Sensitivity Analysis of Occupation Selection}

In the maximum-area triangle search, exhaustive enumeration of all occupation combinations has computational complexity $O(n^3)$, where $n$ is the total number of occupations. When the O*NET database contains over 1,000 occupations, this brute-force enumeration is too time-consuming and resource-intensive. To address this, we adopt a ``candidate pruning'' strategy: for each domain (STEM/Trade/Arts), we select the top $M$ occupations farthest from the domain center, plus the most extreme occupations on each feature axis, forming a candidate pool. We only perform the triple loop search within this candidate pool.

\paragraph{Validation Method}
To verify that pruning does not miss the true optimal solution, we perform a sensitivity scan on candidate pool size $M$. Specifically, we take $M\in\{200,400,800\}$ across three tiers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $M=200$: Compact candidate pool with highest computational efficiency;
    \item $M=400$: Candidate pool expanded by a factor of two, covering more edge-case occupations;
    \item $M=800$: Candidate pool approaches full domain occupation size, approximating complete enumeration.
\end{itemize}

The solution results for the maximum-area triangle under the three candidate pool sizes are shown in Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity_M}.

\subsection{Structural Sensitivity of Employment Destination $O$}

The employment axis's sole exogenous semantic input is the target occupation set $O$. If $O$'s definition scope changes, $J(o)$, $v_{o,j}$, and $\Delta E_m$ will undergo systematic shifts, thereby altering the meaning of ``labor market match rate.'' To test the model's cross-institutional extrapolation boundaries, we vary only $O$, fixing $H_m$, $A_m$, $A_m^{\mathrm{strict}}$, and the quantile threshold strategy, with $w_o$ set to uniform distribution.

We construct three scope tiers based on O*NET's Related Occupations (Primary-Short/Primary-Long): $O_{\text{narrow}}$ as the most direct SOC, $O_{\text{medium}}$ adding 1--2 neighboring SOCs, and $O_{\text{wide}}$ further expanding by 2--4 related SOCs. Based on this, we establish extrapolation boundaries: when $J$ is high, core reform recommendations are primarily driven by AI-overlap, with strong cross-institutional generalizability; when $\tau$ significantly decreases, the employment axis's dependence on $O$ increases, and extrapolation requires re-specifying $O$'s definition scope.
